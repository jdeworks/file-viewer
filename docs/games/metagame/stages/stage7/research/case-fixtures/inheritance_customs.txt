CUSTOM OF THE MERIDIAN ESTATE — ON RECOGNISED HEIRS
(a note for the solicitor, drawn from the family's long usage)

The estate has always followed the older custom, which is wider than the strict
letter of the law:

  · A child of the blood is an heir, whether born within the marriage or a
    NATURAL child openly ACKNOWLEDGED by the late master. An acknowledged
    natural daughter is a recognised claimant and her claim is not irregular.

  · First and second cousins, nephews and nieces, may claim where the will or a
    codicil names them.

  · A claim recognised by custom still stands or falls on the RECORDS. Custom
    admits a claimant to be heard; it does not excuse a false account of money
    paid or promised.

In short: an unusual kinship is not, by itself, a mark against a claimant.
